Stage 3 · Logo analysis

The logo works, but its file format forces three decisions

Supplied file: JPEG, 1254 × 1254 px, 99 KB, opaque white background, stacked lockup (shield emblem above wordmark above tagline). I have not altered it.

1. Header size

The lockup is almost square, so it is tall, not wide. Shown here at 150 px wide (about a 72 px tall header logo). The wordmark cap height is only about 12 px. At the 56 px mobile size it drops to about 9 px.

Proposal: 76 px logo in a 92 px header on desktop, 60 px on mobile. Ask your designer for an SVG or horizontal lockup, which would fix this properly.

2. Dark backgrounds

The white background is baked into the pixels. On navy sections the logo needs a white plate behind it, which looks heavy and reads as a sticker.

Proposal: keep the logo on white or very light surfaces only. Footer becomes a light footer, or a navy footer with one white logo plate. Ask for a transparent PNG or SVG to remove the problem.

3. Whitespace trim

The file has about 3% white margin on the sides and top and about 11% below the tagline. Trimming it is a crop of empty pixels only, not artwork. Every mockup on this page uses that trim.

Needs your approval because the PRD says not to modify the JPEG. Trim done with CSS, the file stays untouched.

Please send, if they exist: SVG, or transparent PNG, of the logo; and ideally a horizontal lockup (emblem left, wordmark right) and the emblem alone. Until then I use the supplied JPEG exactly as given, with the CSS trim above if you agree.

Stage 3 · Why these colours

Colour choices matched to who is deciding

Blue is the most widely trusted colour in security, finance and construction. The logo’s orange and green are brighter than most of those brands use, so the job of the palette is to put calm blue in charge and let the warm colours do one thing each.

Builders and developers

They are judging: competence and scale. Palette response: deep navy, tight grid, blueprint lines. A planning-led look reads as engineering rigour, which is exactly what a developer wants from a surveillance partner.

Housing society committees

They are judging: accountability. A committee has to justify its choice to residents. Palette response: calm blue, lots of white, a visible step-by-step process. Nothing that looks like fear marketing.

Homeowners and flat owners

They are judging: “will this make my home feel safer, and are these people approachable?” Palette response: light sky-blue surfaces and open white space. Green marks the long-term support promise as “ongoing care”, not as a sales colour.

Shops, offices, warehouses

They are judging: speed and practicality. Palette response: one clear action per screen. Orange is the highest-visibility safety hue, so it marks the single thing to do next, never decoration.

What to avoid

  • Red and black-heavy dark UI. Alarm and “spying” associations. YONAM’s promise is protection and planning.
  • Using all six logo colours equally. It looks like a rainbow brand, which weakens trust.
  • Neon cyan glow. Reads as gaming or crypto, not a property-security partner.

A fair caution

Colour associations are tendencies, they vary by culture, and they do not guarantee more enquiries. The real conversion drivers are clear copy, a visible next step and genuine proof. I recommend a small A/B test on the primary button colour once the site has traffic (royal versus orange with navy text).

Stage 3 · Typography

One family: Manrope

The YONAM wordmark is a heavy, wide, geometric sans. Manrope’s ExtraBold has the same character, so headlines feel related to the logo. It stays clear at small body sizes, supports tabular figures for phone numbers, and it is a single variable font, so one request.
